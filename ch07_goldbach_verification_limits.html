Verified for a Billion Cases — Still Not a Proof
Goldbach's Conjecture: every even number greater than 2 is the sum of two primes. Click to check more — watch the confirmation bar grow toward infinity while never becoming a proof.
The aha: Goldbach's Conjecture has been verified by computer for every even number up to over 4×10¹⁸ — and it is STILL not proven, as of this writing. No finite amount of checking can ever establish a claim about infinitely many numbers.
A related conjecture that WAS proven — the "weak" version
This chapter's example is technically the strong (binary) Goldbach conjecture: every even number > 2 is the sum of TWO primes. There's a related, weaker claim: every odd number > 5 is the sum of THREE primes (the "weak" or "ternary" Goldbach conjecture) — for example, 27 = 3 + 5 + 19. Because it only demands three primes instead of two, it's a genuinely easier target, and in 2013 mathematician Harald Helfgott actually proved it, completely and rigorously, for every odd number without exception. This is worth knowing precisely because it shows the "verified but not proven" situation you just explored isn't a permanent feature of every hard conjecture — sometimes the right proof technique arrives and a conjecture becomes a theorem. The strong version above is still waiting for its Helfgott.
